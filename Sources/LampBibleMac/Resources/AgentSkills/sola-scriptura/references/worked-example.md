# A worked example

One real finding and two real non-findings, from an exhortation on 2 Samuel 12 and
Judas. They show the register: the text quoted, the gap named, the smallest remedy —
and, just as important, claims checked and left alone. Every lookup here uses modules
that come with Lamp, so it is reproducible in any library.

## A finding

> "And that contrast brings into focus the dire necessity of our weekly memorial
> around this table as taught in Hebrews, but also of reading the word daily."

Hebrews 10:24–25, the only candidate (`KJVs`):

> And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not
> forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but
> exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.

**Verdict: imported. Load: supporting** — the paragraph's point is the necessity of an
outside voice, which survives without the attribution.

Hebrews teaches assembling, and gives a reason for it. It does not mention the
memorial, the bread and wine, or any frequency. The memorial is instituted at Luke
22:19 and 1 Corinthians 11:23–26, and the first-day gathering to break bread is Acts
20:7 — which is also where `tske` sends you from Hebrews 10:25, its references under
*forsaking* including Acts 2:42, Acts 20:7 and 1 Corinthians 11:17–20. So the practice
is scriptural and the attribution is not: *as taught in Hebrews* names the one text in
the chain that does not teach it.

**Remedy — cite the text that does carry it:** "our weekly memorial around this table,
as Paul received it and delivered it (1 Corinthians 11:23–26), and as the disciples at
Troas kept it on the first day of the week (Acts 20:7)". The sentence keeps its force
and stops resting on Hebrews.

## Two non-findings

**A lexical claim that checks out.** "the chronicler's summary … uses the same word
we've just been looking at in 2 Samuel 12 … Despising his words." Both are H959 —
2 Samuel 12:9 *despised* and 2 Chronicles 36:16 *and despised* in the `KJVs` Strong's
annotations, which `strongs_hebrew` gives as *bāzāh*, "to disesteem; despise, disdain".
Verified, so say nothing. A writer who gets no credit for the claims that hold will not
trust the audit on the one that fails.

**A named external label.** "This is sometimes called a juridical parable, which just
means it's a parable designed to get the defendant to pass sentence on himself."
External, and the writer has marked it external twice over — *sometimes called*, and a
definition rather than an appeal. Nothing to find; see `false-positives.md`.

## What the example is for

The finding was not reached by noticing that Hebrews felt wrong. It was reached by
looking up every citation in the piece, including the ones that were obviously fine,
and asking of each whether the text carries the weight. Twelve citations verified
clean; one did not. That ratio is normal and it is the point — the audit's value is in
the pass, not in the hunch.
