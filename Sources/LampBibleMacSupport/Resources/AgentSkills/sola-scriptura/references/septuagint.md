# Reading the Septuagint

Read this before citing `LXX` or `Brenton`. Both are bundled candidates, but
`list_modules` decides availability and permission. `LXX` is Brenton's Greek OT;
`Brenton` is his English of that Greek. Neither has Strong's tagging.

They show a Greek rendering of the Hebrew, not a second independent scriptural
witness to it or a lexicon definition. A claim dependent on that rendering must
name the dependence. Where an NT quotation follows its wording, compare the actual
texts before saying so; that is evidence about the NT author's use of the OT, not
an extra witness to the OT claim. A difference from `TAHOT` alone cannot establish
whether a translator chose differently or had a different underlying Hebrew text.

`Brenton` supplies `{kind: "added"}` annotations and translator footnotes through
`read_study_material`. Attribute a point resting on supplied English to Brenton.

## Numbering is not converted

`read_passage` uses each module's own reference and does not align parallel texts.
Do not batch it with a Hebrew-numbered translation under an unchecked reference.

- Psalms are not offset by one throughout. English 9–10 are Greek 9, 114–115 are
  Greek 113, English 116 spans Greek 114–115, and English 147 spans Greek 146–147.
  Psalms 148–150 agree again; these modules also contain Psalm 151.
- A psalm's title may occupy verse 1. English Psalm 22:1 is `LXX` 21:2.
- These modules have no separate Nehemiah: it is within Ezra 11–23, so Nehemiah 1:1
  corresponds to Ezra 11:1 here.

Request the corresponding reference separately and check the opening wording and
context before citing it. If correspondence cannot be verified, say so; do not
silently compare two different passages.
